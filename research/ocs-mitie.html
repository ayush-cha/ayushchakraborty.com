Transaction overview

On 21 July 2026, OCS Group International announced a recommended cash acquisition of Mitie Group plc. Mitie shareholders are offered 218.5p in cash and may retain the FY26 final dividend of up to 3.1p per share, giving total headline value of 221.6p. The offer values Mitie's fully diluted equity at approximately £3.1bn.

Investment-banking question

The central question is whether the strategic value of combining OCS and Mitie justifies paying a substantial premium for a high-quality standalone asset, and whether the enlarged group can support the financing required to execute the transaction.

Strategic rationale

The combination creates a significantly larger facilities-management platform with broader geographic reach, sector exposure and technical capability. OCS can gain scale across government, defence, healthcare, infrastructure and other mission-critical environments, while combining technology, customer relationships and operating expertise.

Target quality and premium

Mitie entered the transaction from a position of strength rather than distress. FY26 revenue was £5.619bn, operating profit before Other items was £264m and free cash flow was £162m, alongside a £16.3bn order book. The 218.5p cash consideration represents a 44.7% premium to the 151.0p unaffected share price; including the permitted 3.1p dividend raises headline value to 221.6p and the premium to 46.8%.

Financing and sponsor perspective

OCS is backed by Clayton, Dubilier & Rice. Public financing documents provide for a sponsor contribution framework alongside committed acquisition debt, including bridge, term-loan and revolving facilities. The model therefore focuses on sources and uses, opening leverage, deleveraging and illustrative sponsor returns rather than public-company EPS accretion/dilution.

Merger economics

The accompanying model combines OCS and Mitie operating profiles and tests illustrative synergy, financing and deleveraging scenarios. Synergies and sponsor-return assumptions are clearly separated from disclosed company guidance. The base analytical case uses £100m of illustrative annual synergies to test how execution could affect leverage and value creation.

Conclusion

The transaction has strong industrial logic and combines two scaled facilities-management businesses, but the premium makes execution important. My assessment is that the price is defensible if OCS can capture meaningful operating benefits, integrate Mitie without disrupting contract performance and use cash generation to reduce acquisition leverage. If those benefits disappoint, the substantial premium leaves less room for error.
