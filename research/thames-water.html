Thames Water — Restructuring & Recapitalisation Analysis.

An analysis of whether Thames Water's proposed recapitalisation can restore financial resilience while allocating losses credibly across its capital structure.

Read PDF reportDownload Excel model

Research question: Can Thames Water's proposed recapitalisation reduce leverage enough to restore financial resilience while allocating losses credibly across the capital structure?

Overview

The project examines Thames Water as a distressed restructuring case rather than a conventional corporate-finance transaction. It looks at the company's balance-sheet stress, creditor hierarchy, new-money requirements and the economics of the March 2026 London & Valley Water recapitalisation proposal.

Key transaction terms

The proposal combines £3.35bn of new equity with £3.25bn of day-one debt and up to £3.30bn of committed undrawn debt. It also proposes a 30% write-off of Class A debt, a full write-off of Class B debt and existing subordinated capital, and a 52% day-one net gearing target.

Methodology

The analysis reconstructs the capital structure, separates disclosed proposal terms from illustrative assumptions, and tests the restructuring through a creditor-recovery waterfall, gearing bridge, funding-capacity analysis and haircut sensitivities. The focus is on who absorbs losses, how much new capital is required and whether leverage can remain sustainable through the AMP8 investment programme.

Conclusion

The restructuring is best understood as a balance-sheet reset. Earlier liquidity measures bought time, but the March 2026 proposal attempts to solve the deeper solvency problem by forcing legacy capital to absorb losses and replacing it with a substantially larger equity cushion. The proposed structure creates a more credible starting point, although its long-term success still depends on operating performance, regulatory support and disciplined funding of the investment programme.
