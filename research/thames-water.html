Reported starting position

At 30 September 2025, Thames Water reported statutory net debt of £17,626.9m, covenant net debt of £19,337m, covenant net debt net of TWSSI cash of £18,798m, RCV of £21,893m and senior gearing net of TWSSI cash of 85.9%.

Proposal mechanics

The London & Valley Water proposal announced on 16 March 2026 contemplated £3.35bn of new equity and up to £6.55bn of new debt, comprising £3.25bn of new day-one debt plus up to £3.30bn of committed undrawn debt. It proposed a 30% Class A principal write-off, full impairment of Class B debt and subordinated debt / existing equity, 52% day-one net leverage, leverage rising and stabilising at about 60% by the end of AMP8, and a long-term net-gearing target of 65% or less.

The £3.25bn figure is new day-one debt, not total post-restructuring debt. Reinstated Class A debt must also be included when describing the final capital structure.

RCV & debt-capacity scenario

The model uses the reported £21.893bn RCV. If RCV grows 5% annually for four periods and target gearing rises to 60% by FY30, the resulting illustrative FY30 debt-capacity envelope is approximately £15.97bn. This is a scenario, not a forecast of actual post-restructuring debt.

Nominal principal retention versus economic recovery

A 30% principal write-off means 70% of Class A principal is nominally retained, but economic recovery also depends on the coupon, maturity and required discount rate of the reinstated debt. The workbook therefore includes a present-value sensitivity rather than equating retained principal with recovery value. In an illustrative £15bn claim / 6% coupon / 10-year maturity / 8% discount-rate case, economic value is £9.09bn, or 60.6% of the original claim.

Creditor-priority framing

The creditor-priority framing is that junior claims should not retain value while senior claims remain impaired, subject to the actual restructuring terms.
