Historical basis

FY26 D&A is ₹1,710.53 crore, FY26 PAT attributable to owners is ₹20,689.47 crore, FY26 inventory is ₹18,622.96 crore, and FY26 net working capital is ₹16,906.27 crore under the model’s stated definition of inventory + receivables − payables.

Forecast construction

The model starts from FY26 segment revenue and PBIT, forecasts each segment separately, applies an explicit intersegment-revenue elimination, adds other operating revenue and bridges segment PBIT to consolidated operating EBIT. Consolidated revenue rises from ₹89,913.33 crore in FY26A to ₹125,437.1 crore in FY31E in the central scenario.

Valuation

At 9.5% WACC and 4.5% terminal growth, the central illustrative DCF is ₹362.8 per share. The valuation bridge uses ₹21,360.4 crore of net liquid financial assets and ₹5,288.2 crore of equity-accounted investments at carrying value. Against the retained ₹263.50 portfolio reference price, the mathematical gap is 37.7%; it is not presented as a price target or recommendation.

Econometric work

The regression uses 64 supplied monthly ITC/NIFTY return observations from May 2021 to August 2026. The RBI policy-rate series uses the published change dates: 6.50% from February 2023, 6.00% from April 2025 and 5.50% from June 2025. September 2026 is excluded because the report date falls within the month.

The regression gives a repo coefficient of 0.1123, NIFTY coefficient of 0.9513 and R² of 0.3682 using HC1 robust covariance and normal-approximation p-values. It is exploratory association analysis, not a causal estimate. The supplied return observations themselves are not independently certified because the underlying price observations, adjustment factors and extraction timestamps were not supplied.

Current operating context

ITC's 31 July 2026 Q1 FY27 statement reported consolidated FMCG segment revenue growth of 15% YoY and segment results growth of 22%, while consolidated EBITDA and PAT before exceptional items were lower by 24% and 23% YoY. The forecast therefore treats cigarette-margin recovery as an assumption rather than mechanically extrapolating prior profitability.
