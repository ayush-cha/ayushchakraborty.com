How Singapore finances public investment

Tax revenue, public wealth and lessons for the United Kingdom.

Why can Singapore fund extensive infrastructure while keeping personal income tax relatively low? This project looks beyond headline tax rates to public investment returns, state-owned assets and compulsory household saving, then compares Singapore’s approach with the UK’s revenue base and spending commitments. The next stage will test how these financing arrangements translate into infrastructure outcomes and productivity.

Financial regulation and competitive balance in English football

An IB World Studies Extended Essay: an interdisciplinary research paper combining mathematics and economics to investigate a global issue through a local case study. This essay examines whether UEFA’s Financial Fair Play guidelines and the Premier League’s Profit and Sustainability Rules support competitive balance and long-term financial viability.

It combines economic theories of market structure and barriers to entry with the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index, concentration ratios and a competitive-balance measure based on points and adjusted goal difference. The analysis finds no clear sustained improvement in competitive balance after regulation, and considers whether financial safeguards can also reinforce established clubs’ advantages.

How fintech and digital currencies are reshaping banks and payments

A university essay examining how fintech lenders, digital payment platforms and central bank digital currencies challenge traditional deposit-taking institutions. It connects financial-intermediation theory with a critical review of academic research and policy proposals, including the digital pound.

Using UK Faster Payments and Bacs statistics alongside World Bank financial-inclusion data, the paper explores changes in payment activity and access to finance. It weighs the potential for faster services and wider inclusion against regulatory arbitrage, risks to bank deposit funding and persistent barriers to access.
